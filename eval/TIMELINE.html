PHASE 03 2026-09-14 → 09-17

Query decomposition, falsified three times

The hypothesis: splitting a compound question into sub-questions raises recall on the questions that need two or three source articles. It got three independent tests, each fairer than the last.

Test 1 · n=50

Turn it on and measure

regression
ResultEvery retrieval metric fell. context_recall 0.704 → 0.533, hit_rate 0.760 → 0.620, answer_relevancy 0.648 → 0.546. Even precision@k — the metric decomposition was built to raise — went down. Only faithfulness ticked up.
Root cause · revised twice

Hairline scores, and what they turned out to mean

diagnosis revised
Read 1Fragments replace the holistic query's search instead of adding to it, so chunks the full question found well are dropped by the score threshold.
Read 2Score data killed that. MIN_SCORE_THRESHOLD = 0.35 never fires in production — all 120 observed scores sit between 0.6398 and 0.8304, and every question fills all six slots. Nothing is ever filtered; the contest is purely ordinal, and the margins are hairline.

It was never out-ranked. The merge kept the max score and sorted descending, so had the gold chunk surfaced in either fragment's results at anything ≥ 0.6583 it would have ranked first. Every ON score falls below every OFF score because the fragment searches never retrieved it at all. The holistic query is never searched, so anything only the whole question would have found is unreachable — and with all six slots always full, that loss is total and silent.
The proofOn q007 and q012 the fragment scores came back higher than the holistic ones — q007 ON at 0.7574–0.7867 against OFF at 0.7500–0.7743 — while retrieving worse context. Higher number, worse answer. These are cosine scores against different query vectors, so the scale never carried across them, which is exactly the assumption _fuse() was built on.
ConsequenceSaturation is the design, not the defect — so no fix may try to relieve it. Widening top_k games the @k metric; budgeting slots per subquery makes q004 worse by construction; reserving the holistic top-m fails because q004's gold ranked 5th of 6. Plain RRF fails too: gold at holistic rank 5 with no fragment support scores 1/(60+5) = 0.0154 and loses to anything ranking 3rd in two fragment lists at 2/(60+3) = 0.0317.
The ruleFragments decide what gets considered; the original question decides what wins. Every contender measured against one vector — the user's actual question.
The tests encoded the wrong mechanism. All three regression tests made the threshold the decisive lever — a lever production never pulls — and none of them saturated top_k, so the crowding-out path was literally unreachable. They asserted presence; production asks about survival against 23 competitors for 6 slots. They would have passed a fix that doesn't work, which is worse than having no tests at all.
Test 2 · n=10

The additive fix — goal met on iteration 1 of 3

goal met
BlockerRaised before burning iterations: all ten smoke questions are single-source, and decomposition-OFF already scores 1.000 on hit_rate and recall there — a ceiling that can be tied but not beaten. The run went ahead with success redefined as "same or better".
ChangeAlways search the original query, widened to top_k × HOLISTIC_OVERFETCH = 48; rank candidates by their score against the real question; delete _fuse(), which encoded the assumption that cosine scores are comparable across different queries.
ResultEvery metric at or above baseline — but retrieval came back byte-identical to OFF on 10/10 questions, so the faithfulness gain could only be answerer nondeterminism, not the fix. Reported as such rather than banked.

The bridge

Why a cross-encoder, specifically

direction set
TheoryA cross-encoder scores the (query, chunk) pair jointly, in one forward pass, instead of comparing two embeddings computed independently of one another. That independence is the same defect already diagnosed upstream — it is why fragment scores of 0.7867 beat holistic scores of 0.7743 while retrieving worse context, and why a compound question's second topic gets buried. If independently-computed similarity is the weakness, joint scoring is the direct answer to it.
Practicalitysentence-transformers was already a dependency and ships cross-encoders — no new stack, no re-ingest — and the 48-candidate pool was already being fetched. The first experiment cost nothing but a model download and a read-only simulation.
SequenceNamed as a one-line candidate when the n=22 arms came back null, then parked — "finish current work" — and picked up a session later: "do a simulation of the reranking using your suggestion of query and chunk."

Cross-encoder reranking — and one false positive on the way

A cross-encoder scores the query and the chunk jointly, rather than comparing two embeddings computed in isolation — precisely the weakness that buries a compound question's second topic.

ResultReturns are near-linear at ~38 ms per pair, so halving the latency halves the benefit. Twelve candidates scores below doing nothing — too shallow a pool means the reranker demotes correct chunks without better alternatives to promote. Going deeper hurts too: more distractors to misrank. 48 or nothing.
ProvenanceAsked where 48 came from, the honest answer was: arbitrary. It was top_k × 8 from the decomposition fix — "start at 8" — and every later experiment inherited it because it was already being fetched. The rank-band diagnostic justified it afterwards (48 captures 76% of gold, 12 captures 51%), and latency caps the usable range anyway. Best tested value, not a proven optimum.
PHASE 05 2026-09-17 → 09-18

Making it deployable on one 16 GB card

The quality case was settled at n=32. The deployment case — latency, VRAM, cold start — was the harder half, and it surfaced a bug the eval could never have caught.

Part A

Ship the reranker; verify against the prototype

shipped
Result401 tests green, and the shipped retrieve() reproduced the A2 prototype numbers to four decimals. It also surfaced two real CUDA OOM warnings — 171 MB free on a 16 GB card — and showed that of 4.20 s of retrieval, decomposition was 2.2 s of it, the larger half, buying nothing.
VRAM

Smaller batches are both leaner and faster

framing corrected
ResultThe default batch of 32 was the worst cell on both axes: with variable-length inputs every pair pads to the longest chunk in the batch. Batch 8 saves 681 MiB of peak VRAM and ~137 ms. And expandable_segments:True addresses the real OOM cause — a 365 MB allocation failing with 171 MB free is fragmentation, not exhaustion.
RetractedThe earlier "~14.7 GB permanently co-resident" warning was wrong: nothing is permanently resident. Ollama unloads after a five-minute keep-alive, and all three models coexist only during an active query.
Pool depth

A bug found by reading code, because no measurement could find it

fixed
BugPool size was decided by whether decomposition happened to fire: 48 candidates if the question split, 12 if it didn't. The reranker's needs never entered into it.
Why invisibleNo question in the test set is under six words — the shortest is 11, the median 29 — because RAGAS writes verbose synthetic questions. Real people type "why is gold up?". Every eval question takes the 48 path, so no measurement on this test set can detect the problem.
The trapDisabling a feature that measurably does nothing would have silently dropped every query to 12 candidates — worse than no reranking at all — and it would have looked like the reranker's fault.
Part B · n=50

Decomposition's last test, then off

disabled
ResultAcross all 50 questions the shipped merge differs from not decomposing at all on zero of them. Repaired variants gain a net one question in fifty — two better, one worse, a coin flip. RRF and simple slot reservation produce identical metrics, so the fusion machinery and its tuning parameter buy nothing over holding 12 slots open.
VerdictTechnically it passed the pre-registered criterion — which was then stated plainly to have been written without a minimum effect size, and this effect falls below what it implicitly assumed was meaningful. Disabled: ~2.2 s on 94% of queries for a coin-flip-level gain.
Cold start

The embedder costs more than the reranker

35.4 s → 15.4 s
MeasuredBGE embedder first load 11.56 s; reranker load 6.13 s; Qdrant first search 1.19 s; Ollama completion 3.92 s. The expectation had been the reverse. ~18.9 s is once per process; only ~3.7 s recurs.
DecisionSynchronous in-process pre-warm behind a visible spinner — an honest "Loading models…" beats a fast-looking app that then hangs for 20 s. Raising OLLAMA_KEEP_ALIVE was rejected: ~9 GB held permanently to save 3.7 s re-creates exactly the headroom pressure just resolved.

The bottleneck has moved from retrieval to generation. All three answering metrics sit inside the ±0.05 threshold set in advance — and the two scoring passes were unusually stable, with context_precision, context_recall and answer_relevancy identical to four decimals, against ±0.107 of wobble at n=22. Further retrieval work (the remaining 0.10 oracle gap, a recency prior, chunking) now has clearly diminishing returns; the constraint is the answerer, or the answer prompt.

What the log is really about

Measuring only where an intervention should help is how you get a false positive

It happened twice, one turn apart — first with decomposition on the multi-source slice, then with the reranker on the same slice, by the same author who had just criticised it. Three devices eventually stopped it: a single-source guard on every measurement, an effect-size threshold written down before the run, and an oracle ceiling to say how much of the gap was ever reachable.
